### A1. Methods (exact)

| Component | What they did |
|---|---|
| Corpus | DEBAGREEMENT (Pougué-Biyong et al. 2021): 42,894 Reddit comment-reply pairs across five subreddits, each labelled agree, neutral or disagree. They use the r/Brexit subset: 15,745 pairs, of which **5,555 have unanimous annotator agreement**. Grouped into 30 monthly windows (Dec 2018 to May 2021). |
| Windows kept | 24 (Jan 2019 to Jan 2021). A window is kept only if the continuous graph has at least one cycle and the human-label graph has at least three independent cycles. Nov 2019, Feb 2021, Mar 2021 and May 2021 are forests; Dec 2018 and Apr 2021 have one cycle. Nov 2019 is the only gap inside the series. |
| Nodes, edges | Nodes are users. An undirected edge joins two users who exchanged a comment and reply. Per-window counts are not reported. Mean degree is about 2.1 to 3.1, which is very sparse. |
| Edge sign | Stance model: LoRA-adapted Llama-3.2-1B over {disagree, neutral, agree}. Per exchange, w = (p_agree - p_disagree)(1 - p_neutral). The edge weight is the mean w over a pair's exchanges. A second construction uses the human labels (-1/0/+1) and takes the sign of the mean. |
| Stance model quality | Held-out accuracy 84.5%, weighted F1 0.846. No per-class F1. Data were split chronologically 80/10/10, with training ending 6 Dec 2020. **About 87% of the analysed Brexit interactions fall inside the training period**, so most edge signs are in-sample predictions. |
| Polarisation measures | Frustration index F, the minimum number of frustrated edges over a two-camp partition, found by multistart descent with basin-hopping (20,000 restarts). The values are numerical upper bounds. Count index P^c = 1 - F/κ, where κ = m - n + 1 is the cyclomatic number of the largest component. Weighted index P^w = 1 - F^w/C^w. Comparison measures: edge-normalised 1 - 2F/m, Eigen-Sign, and generalised spectral. |
| Measure validation | Synthetic signed SBM: 80 nodes, mean degree 2 to 24, sign-flip probability η from 0 to 0.5, 600 graphs. Spearman correlation with η is -0.77 for P^c and P^w, against -0.47 to -0.60 for the alternatives. On a size/density screen, Eigen-Sign (ρ with edge count -0.989) and edge-normalised frustration (ρ with mean degree -0.941) fail. Test-retest ICC (95% edge subsamples): P^c 0.71, P^w 0.70. Sign-shuffle null (200 replicates): only 2 of 24 months exceed the 95th percentile for P^w, and 4 for P^c. |
| Language features | (1) A **scalar-intensity lexicon generated by GPT-5**: five runs of one prompt with ten calibration anchors, giving 100 to 139 terms per run, 44 terms common to all five, and pairwise Jaccard 0.41 to 0.51. The lexicon includes intensifiers, emotion words, **Brexit stance terms ("remainer", "brexiteer") and charged descriptors ("corrupt")**. Each pair takes its maximum term score. (2) Toxicity: unitary/toxic-bert, as the share of "high-toxicity" interactions (threshold not stated), the monthly mean and the monthly maximum. (3) GPT-2 perplexity. |
| Tests | Contemporaneous Pearson and Spearman correlations with a circular-shift p (21 offsets). Covariate-adjusted OLS. Nowcasting and forecasting with a random forest (500 trees) on P_{t-1}, rolling origin: 22 month pairs, 10 burn-in, 12 evaluation months. Secondary check: chronological 80/20 split. |

### A3. Limitations

**Stated by the authors:** two-camp partition only; one platform, one topic, unanimous pairs only; 6 of 30 windows excluded, leaving 12 evaluation months; the lag-1 null applies at monthly resolution only; weak sensitivity at mean degree 2 to 3, with upper-bound optimisation; lexicon tested for sampling robustness only (not prompt robustness, not against human judgement); no causal or temporal-precedence claim; "associations may partly reflect shared linguistic cues".

**Additional (mine; argue with these):**
1. **The sign means disagreement, not hostility.** Frustration here measures how far opinion splits into two camps, not relational antagonism. Respectful disagreement is a negative edge. This is the opening for N2: your category 3 (non-punitive moral criticism) is exactly a disagree-but-not-punitive edge, and their design cannot separate it.
2. **The lexicon leaks stance.** "Remainer", "brexiteer" and "corrupt" are scalar terms in their lexicon, and stance also defines the edges. So part of r = +0.56 may be stance predicting stance-structure. It also overlaps with Rathje-style out-group reference.
3. **Filtering to unanimous labels removes the ambiguous middle.** Keeping only the 5,555 pairs all annotators agreed on drops 65% of the data, most likely the implicit, hedged exchanges. Your construct (implicit punitive) lives there. Their toxicity null may partly reflect this filter combined with a moderated subreddit, which leaves little toxicity variance. It is weak evidence for "toxicity misses it", not strong evidence.
4. **Edge signs are mostly in-sample.** With 87% training overlap, measured edge quality is optimistic.
5. **n = 24 months, three language families, several aggregations.** No multiple-comparison correction is visible. The headline P^c association depends on one month.
6. **Tiny graphs.** On average about 185 unanimous pairs per month, in the sparse regime where their own SBM test shows weak sensitivity.

### A4. What a Bluesky study signing edges by a theory-derived punitive construct adds

| Their design | Your delta | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Sign = stance (agree/disagree) | Sign = punitiveness (codebook, -2 to +3), with stance and toxicity signing run **on the same edge set** | Separates "disagreement splits networks" from "punitive disagreement splits networks". Disagree-but-non-punitive edges (cat. 3) are the decisive contrast. |
| GPT-5 lexicon, no human validation | Codebook derived from theory (Fehr-Gächter traces, deservingness, Gottman), human gold set, Krippendorff's α, distilled encoder | Construct validity they explicitly lack |
| One subreddit, Brexit, 24 monthly points | Multi-topic Bluesky with topic and blame-target edge tags, weekly or daily windows | Power, generality across topics, spillover |
| Mean degree 2 to 3 | Denser firehose graphs, with quotes as well as replies | Moves out of the sparse regime; makes multi-block measures (SPONGE) feasible, which they list as future work |
| Language feature and edge sign come from the same text | **Risk you inherit and must design out:** if punitiveness both signs the edges and "explains" frustration, the result is circular | Score M3 on an **independent outcome** (future blocking, as in Bono et al. 2026; unfollow or exit; decay of cross-group replies; frustration on a held-out later window built from likes or follows), not on frustration of the same graph |

### A5. How their forecasting null bears on H7, and a design that would answer it

**Bearing (partial, not fatal).**
- Their outcome is structural frustration of a stance network. H7's outcome is a *post-event surge in explicit punitive expression*, which is a different variable. The null does not directly falsify H7.
- What it does show is that **calendar-month language does not predict next-month structure** in one sparse community. The polarisation series is close to noise month to month (lag-1 r = -0.13). With 12 evaluation months, a real lagged effect would be almost undetectable. Their null is low-powered absence of evidence.
- The real warning for H7 is different. If pre-event implicit punitiveness predicts post-event explicit surges, the obvious rival is **persistence**: punitive communities stay punitive. H7 needs to show that implicit markers add predictive value over pre-event explicit level, toxicity, outrage and volume.

**Design that would answer it.**
1. **Event-locked, pooled event study.** Pre-register K shocks (aim for at least 10, chosen before seeing data, coded by blame target). For each community c and event e, the predictor is implicit punitive prevalence in [-14, -1] days and the outcome is the rise in explicit punitive prevalence in [0, +7] days. This gives power across communities × events, not one time series.
2. **Baselines in the same model:** pre-event explicit level, Perspective, Brady's outrage classifier, Simchon et al.'s polarisation dictionary, posting volume, and community fixed effects.
3. **Placebo dates** (non-event windows) and permutation of event dates for inference, mirroring their circular-shift test.
4. **Prediction specific to the reservoir:** the effect should be larger for events whose blame target is the out-group, and close to zero for no-wrongdoer shocks (section 8 of the design doc). Toxicity and outrage do not obviously predict this interaction.
5. **Falsification criterion (pre-registered):** H7 fails if the implicit coefficient's 95% CI includes zero once pre-event explicit level is controlled, or if placebo dates show the same effect.
